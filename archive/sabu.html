Hector Xavier Monsegur grew up in the Alfred E. Smith Houses — a public housing project on the Lower East Side of Manhattan. His parents were arrested for drug dealing when he was a child. He was raised by his grandmother. He learned to hack as a teenager, teaching himself on dial-up internet in a city that didn't have much else to offer.

By 2011 he was Sabu — the de facto leader and co-founder of LulzSec, the most notorious hacking collective since Anonymous. Under his leadership, LulzSec hacked Fox News, PBS, Sony, the CIA, the US Senate, and the UK's Serious Organised Crime Agency during a 50-day rampage that made headlines worldwide.

The FBI arrested him on June 7, 2011. He was facing 124 years in prison. His grandmother was elderly. His cousins depended on him. The day after his arrest, he agreed to become an FBI informant.

For the next three years, while his LulzSec co-defendants were being indicted, Sabu worked with the FBI. A court filing later revealed he helped prevent over 300 cyberattacks — including planned attacks on NASA, the US military, and foreign governments. The information he provided led directly to the arrests of his LulzSec colleagues. When sentencing finally came, he served 7 months and was released.

█ The Betrayal — and Why It's Complicated

The hacker community largely views Sabu's FBI cooperation as a betrayal. Jeremy Hammond — who was convicted partly based on information Sabu provided while working as an informant — received a 10-year sentence. Hammond has said publicly that Sabu encouraged the Stratfor hack while already working for the FBI. Sabu was, in effect, an agent provocateur.

Sabu himself has never fully addressed this. His position is that he was protecting his family, his grandmother, and the children in his care. He has acknowledged that some of what LulzSec did "wasn't necessary" and "didn't really prove a point."

The question of whether his cooperation was heroic, cowardly, or simply human is one lulz-sec.com doesn't pretend to answer. We document the history as it happened.

█ After LulzSec — The White Hat Arc

After his release Monsegur rebuilt entirely. He entered the cybersecurity industry as a penetration tester and vulnerability researcher. His skills — which had once been used to embarrass Sony and the CIA — were now being used to protect the same kinds of organizations from attackers like the old Sabu.

He became Director of Assessment Services at Rhino Security Labs, working with major corporations and government agencies. He speaks internationally on cybersecurity and has become one of the more sought-after voices on the psychology of hackers and the reality of cyber threats.

In 2025, he appeared at Zero Trust World alongside Chris Tarbell — the FBI agent who arrested him in 2011. The two now tour together educating businesses on real-world cybersecurity. The optics are either inspiring or deeply ironic depending on where you stand.
