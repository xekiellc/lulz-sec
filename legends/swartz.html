SWARTZ
Aaron Hillel Swartz
RIP — 1986–2013
14Age when co-created RSS
35 yearsPotential sentence
$1MPotential fines
26Age at death

Aaron Swartz was a prodigy in the truest sense. At 14 he co-authored the RSS 1.0 specification — the technology that powers podcast feeds, news aggregators, and content syndication across the internet. He was a fellow at Harvard's Safra Center for Ethics. He helped build Reddit. He co-founded the organization that became Creative Commons. He was a central figure in the campaign that defeated SOPA — the Stop Online Piracy Act — arguably the most significant internet freedom victory of the 2010s.

In 2011 he used MIT's network to systematically download articles from JSTOR — a digital library of academic journals. The articles were academic research, much of it publicly funded. He believed academic knowledge should be free. JSTOR resolved the matter civilly and declined to press charges. The federal government prosecuted anyway.

He faced 13 felony counts under the Computer Fraud and Abuse Act, up to 35 years in prison, and $1 million in fines. For downloading academic articles that JSTOR didn't want to prosecute him for.

On January 11, 2013, Aaron Swartz took his own life. He was 26 years old.

█ The Prosecution

The federal prosecution of Aaron Swartz was, by almost any reasonable standard, grotesquely disproportionate. He had not stolen money. He had not damaged systems. He had not published the documents — they were sitting on his hard drive. JSTOR, the alleged victim, reached a civil settlement with him and explicitly stated it did not want criminal charges pursued.

US Attorney Carmen Ortiz and prosecutor Stephen Heymann pursued the case aggressively regardless. The prosecution offered plea deals that would have required prison time. Swartz refused to plead guilty to something he did not believe was a crime.

His father Robert Swartz said after his death: "Aaron did not commit suicide. He was killed by the government." The prosecution was widely criticized as an example of prosecutorial overreach — using the vague and sweeping Computer Fraud and Abuse Act as a weapon against someone whose only crime was believing information should be free.

█ What He Built

RSS — the protocol that powers every podcast feed on earth, every news aggregator, every content subscription service. Co-authored at 14.

Reddit — he merged his startup Infogami with Reddit in 2005 and became a co-owner. He later distanced himself from it.

Creative Commons — the licensing framework that allows creators to share work freely. He helped build the technical infrastructure.

Demand Progress — the advocacy organization that led the campaign against SOPA. When SOPA would have allowed the government to censor entire websites, Swartz organized the internet blackout that killed it. Wikipedia went dark. Google put a black box over its logo. The bill was shelved.

The internet as it exists today was shaped significantly by Aaron Swartz. He was 26 when he died.
