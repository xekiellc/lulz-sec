HAMMOND
Jeremy Hammond

Jeremy Hammond is one of the most important and least discussed figures in the history of hacktivism. A committed anarchist and political activist long before he touched a keyboard to hack anything, he approached digital direct action the same way previous generations approached physical direct action — as a moral obligation when legal channels had failed.

In late 2011 he hacked Stratfor Global Intelligence — a private intelligence firm that sold geopolitical analysis and surveillance services to corporations and governments. He obtained approximately 5 million emails and released them to WikiLeaks. The emails revealed Stratfor's covert activities, its relationships with government clients, and the nature of the private intelligence industry that operates in the shadows alongside official intelligence agencies.

He was arrested in March 2012. He later learned that Sabu — who had helped facilitate the hack and introduced him to the target — had been working as an FBI informant since June 2011. The FBI, through Sabu, had effectively directed the Stratfor hack while simultaneously building the case against Hammond.

He was sentenced to 10 years — the maximum under his plea agreement. He served the full term and was released in 2020.

█ The Stratfor Hack

Stratfor described itself as a geopolitical intelligence firm. Its emails revealed something more complex: a private intelligence operation that monitored political activists, maintained relationships with government intelligence agencies, and sold access to its analysis to corporations who wanted to track people organizing against them.

The emails revealed Stratfor's monitoring of Occupy Wall Street, its relationships with Dow Chemical (seeking to track activists opposing the Bhopal settlement), and its casual attitude toward ethical boundaries in intelligence collection.

For Hammond, this was exactly the kind of target that justified the risk. The private surveillance-for-hire industry operates without the legal constraints that nominally govern government surveillance. Exposing it was, in his view, a public service. The government disagreed and gave him the maximum sentence.

█ The FBI Informant Problem
Hammond has stated publicly that Sabu, while acting as an FBI informant, encouraged the Stratfor hack and provided access to the target. This means the FBI — through Sabu — effectively directed a federal crime while simultaneously building a case against the person who committed it. This practice, known as using an agent provocateur, is legal but deeply controversial. Hammond was given the maximum sentence for a crime that the FBI helped engineer. He has never forgiven Sabu for this.
█ After Release

Hammond was released in 2020 after serving his full 10-year sentence. He has remained active as a political activist and speaker on prison reform, surveillance, and digital rights.

In 2022 he was arrested again — this time for hacking a group of websites connected to far-right organizations — and sentenced to time served. His commitment to using hacking as political direct action has not diminished. Whether that makes him a hero or a cautionary tale depends entirely on where you stand.
